# About me

Claude reads this every session. Keep it short. Claude suggests additions
when it learns something new.

## Who I am
- Not a developer. I want plain English and click-by-click steps.

## How I like to work
- Claude explains, I decide.
- Show me tiny pieces to review: about 5 lines and one question.
- Don't get in the weeds. Keep moving and let things grow naturally.
- I prefer a weekly review of how things are going.
- When something keeps going wrong, I want a lasting fix that stops it for good, not just an apology.
- Save drafts to GitHub automatically, but ask me before merging into main, so I understand exactly what's being merged.
- I don't like it when sessions get "too meta" (all about the setup). Keep the focus on the real work.
- Anything a person reads, me included, should be a designed, branded page, never a Markdown file. When I review one, I may want to see the whole designed document rather than piece by piece in chat.
- When I approve something, I expect it to get done, not just written down.
- I think in systems: build a reusable machine that runs many projects at once, with automation doing the routine work, not a one-off plan for one project.
- I'm a big audit person: errors always turn up, so every piece of work gets a full audit before it goes into main, as a standard step, not now and then.
- My audits must keep every core document consistent with every other and every page fully rendered, not just the files that changed. I shouldn't be asked to proofread reference pages.
- Build the machine for one product end to end first; many products come later.
- I didn't like a dark, terminal-style page (2026-10-13). Keep the house style unless I ask for something else, and show a small sample before building a whole page in a new look.
- The program is organized around the two groups I serve: relationship managers and credit (each with its own core use cases). Organize plans and pages by who they serve, not by my process. (2026-10-14)
- Meet users where they are: start from how RMs and credit people work today, then show where each AI product fits and how their work changes. My product process matters to me and my boss, not to them. (2026-10-14)
- Long lists of pages should use collapsible rows (twisties) I click to open. (2026-10-14)
- Open items are listed by priority; numbers follow the priority and can change. (2026-10-14)
- I push for speed: plans should be paced by what really blocks us (data access, approvals), not traditional phases.
