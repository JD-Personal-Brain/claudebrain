Is your bank's frontline team ready?" and checks the RM plan against its five use cases. Checked against the article's text, pasted in by me on 2026-10-10.

The five use cases

#Use caseWhat the AI doesReported impact (McKinsey)
1Prospecting (market maps and next best opportunities)Combines data sources into ranked lists of high-potential clients, refreshed as markets changeAbout 30% pipeline growth and 10% higher revenue; one bank saw twice the conversion rate of traditional leads
2Lead nurturing (including cross-sell)An agent replies to inquiries, sends tailored content and books meetings, passing only qualified leads to the RMTwo to three times more qualified leads; 5% higher conversion (early pilots)
3Account planning and meeting prep (account intelligence)Pulls data from many sources into an account plan or briefing in minutes, with talking pointsAbout 25% less prep time; 10% more time with clients
4Deal structuring and pricing (dynamic deal scoring)Recommends pricing and discounts from past deals and client data, and speeds approvals10% margin gains; one bank cut deal turnaround from five days to two
5Coaching (AI sales coaching)Reviews call transcripts and gives each banker personal guidanceCustomer satisfaction up seven points; new bankers up to speed 20% faster

McKinsey ties these to the stages of a sale: prospecting and deal close (use case 1), outreach (2), early and late-stage conversations (3, with coaching in late stage), and negotiation (4).

How our RM plan covers them

McKinsey use caseIn our RM planCoverage
1. Prospecting and next best opportunitiesWave 3: prospect lookalikes (B8). Wave 1's weekly opportunity list finds next best opportunities in existing clientsPartly. New-client prospecting comes late (wave 3), and McKinsey reports its biggest numbers here
2. Lead nurturing, including cross-sellWave 2: referral prompts (B6) for cross-sell between teams. Nothing contacts prospects on its ownPartly, by design. Our rule is that nothing reaches a client without the RM, so an agent that writes to prospects by itself is out of scope today
3. Account intelligence and meeting prep (McKinsey: account planning and meeting prep)Wave 1: pre-meeting client brief (B2). Wave 3: RM assistant (B7)Covered, and first in line
4. Deal scoring and pricing (McKinsey: deal structuring and pricing)Not in the plan. The plan keeps the AI away from pricing and credit decisions (control K3)Not covered, by design. Pricing touches fair-lending rules, so it would need a much higher bar
5. AI sales coachingNot in the plan. The onboarding coach (A6) answers "how do I use this?" onlyNot covered

Also in our plan, not in McKinsey's five: early warning when a client may leave (B5), and the meeting follow-up (B3) and call notes saved to the CRM (B4), both in wave 1, which tackle McKinsey's number one problem, excessive admin.

What this means: wave 1 lines up with McKinsey's most-cited problem (meeting prep and admin). The gaps are coaching, which is low risk and could fit an early wave, and prospecting, which McKinsey rates highest for revenue and we have in wave 3. Autonomous lead nurturing and pricing are left out on purpose, for governance reasons. Each open question is a decision in the Open Items log (D6 to D8).

What it changed: the RM plan now uses McKinsey's framework (decided 2026-10-10)

The RM plan is now organized on seven core use cases, with our AI products underneath each one and a return profile for each (the plan's "Core use cases" section): McKinsey's five plus two of our own, admin off the RM's plate (McKinsey's top problem, which its five use cases don't name) and keep clients. Open questions:

  • Prospecting earlier? (D7) McKinsey's biggest revenue numbers are in prospecting, and our prospect lookalikes come in wave 3. Outside prospect data may set the pace.
  • Add coaching? (D6) Low risk, uses call transcripts the meeting features already need, and McKinsey reports faster ramp-up for new bankers.
  • Autonomous lead nurturing and pricing stay out for now? (D8) An agent that writes to prospects on its own, or recommends prices, conflicts with "nothing reaches a client without the RM" and the fair-lending control (K3).

McKinsey's five principles for capturing the value

  1. Define the business model and the value it should create.
  2. Rework a frontline area end to end, not piece by piece, with a repeatable playbook for scaling.
  3. Build reusable technology: an orchestration layer for agents, a data foundation (an "ontology" of the bank's workflows, decisions and knowledge), and trust and security.
  4. Put governance in place: how much each agent may do on its own, monitoring and audit.
  5. Upskill the workforce, as RM roles change.

Where our framework already does this: 1 is the value model and the sponsor's outcome (Discover); 2 is the six-step process and the playbook; 3 is the agent library, the data contract and the Glossary (our working vocabulary, which plugs into the bank's ontology); 4 is the Controls Catalog and "assistive first"; 5 is Release and adopt (training on users' own work and champions). ⚠️ Role redesign (new roles such as "agent orchestrator") isn't covered yet.
