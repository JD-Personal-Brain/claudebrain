Glossary: the words we use

One meaning for each word in every plan, playbook page and agent spec, plus the bank's terms still to map.

One meaning for each word, used the same way in every plan, playbook page and agent spec. The audit checks the core documents against it.

Two kinds of terms. The first three sections are our own working words for how products are delivered: the bank won't supply these, so we define them. The last section lists the bank's business terms our plans depend on. Those are not defined here: each one is marked ⚠️ until the bank's own definition replaces it, because the bank's own names and definitions are the ones we plug into.

How the work is organized

TermWhat it means
StepOne of the six parts every product goes through: Discover, Define, Build, Govern and test, Release and adopt, Measure and iterate.
Sub-stepA numbered piece of a step, from 1.0 to 6.6 (44 in all). Each has its own section in the playbook and its own card in the Steps Catalog.
GateThe go/no-go decision at the end of each step. A named person decides, and the decision goes in the decision log.
Governance threadThe risk and control work that runs through every step, so that step 4 confirms what risk partners have already seen.
Core use caseOne of the seven needs a plan's AI products answer (in the RM plan, each of The RM Assistant's capabilities answers one). The RM plan set them: McKinsey's five frontline use cases (prospecting and next best opportunities, lead nurturing, account intelligence and meeting prep, deal scoring and pricing, AI sales coaching) plus two of our own (admin off the RM's plate, keep clients). The credit work is proposed to reuse the same seven needs through each one's credit counterpart (the Use Case Library's portfolio manager section; used in the PM plan's first cut since 2026-10-19, not yet reviewed). Say RM core use case or credit core use case when it matters which. Each has a user problem and a return profile.
Credit counterpartThe credit-side version of an RM core use case, for portfolio managers and credit analysts; for example, credit memo and annual review drafts under account intelligence and meeting prep (see the Use Case Library's portfolio manager section).
Use case (on its own)A whole area of work for one user group, such as the RM use case or the credit use case. Not one AI product (say AI product) and not one of the seven lines (say core use case).
User workflowHow a user group (RMs, portfolio managers) works today, and how that work changes with AI. Not the same as the plans' numbered workflows (B1 to B9 for RMs, B10 to B16 for PMs) on the Workflow Maps.
AI productA specific capability we build and release, such as the pre-meeting client brief. Each sits under one core use case.
RM AssistantThe core RM product (a hypothesis since 2026-10-17): one personal AI assistant for each RM, with limits on its jobs, workflows, data and controls, and capabilities added feature wave by feature wave on a shared foundation (the RM Assistant page). The RM plan's "RM assistant" (wave 3) is now its Client Q&A capability.
PM AssistantThe core portfolio manager product (a first cut since 2026-10-19): one personal AI assistant for each PM, the credit counterpart of the RM Assistant, with the same kinds of limits and capabilities added feature wave by feature wave (The PM Assistant page). It gathers, drafts and flags; the PM writes the judgment and a named credit officer decides.
CapabilityOne specific job an assistant does, such as the RM Assistant's pre-meeting client brief or the PM Assistant's borrower file. Each capability is one AI product.
Industry background draftThe PM Assistant's first capability: the industry background section of a credit memo (the borrower's industry: its size and trend, main drivers and risks, outlook), drafted from approved and licensed sources only, every statement cited and dated, for the PM to edit and approve. It uses no borrower data and writes no judgment about the borrower. An outside firm the bank pays writes this section today (the credit lead, 2026-10-19; ⚠️ details to confirm).
Borrower fileThe PM Assistant's wave 1 capability that gathers everything about one borrower (financials, loans, covenants, deposits, payments, the last review, the RM's notes) in one place, every figure with its source, and a list of what's missing or late.
Early warning flagsThe PM Assistant's feature wave 2 capability: flags on a borrower, with the numbers behind each, for the PM to look at; never a watchlist or rating change. Named so it can't be mistaken for the bank's watchlist.
Signals engineSimple, explainable rules that spot opportunities or risks, with AI explaining each one; shared by both assistants.
Must-refuse caseA test case the product must refuse, such as a request for a credit decision, a borrower risk rating or a price; required on the higher-risk path.
Judgment sectionsThe parts of a credit draft only the PM writes: the borrower risk rating and, in wave 1, the recommendation.
Content ownerThe person who signs which documents a product may read, such as the credit file's owner; part of the data contract (sub-step 2.3).
WaveUsed two ways, so say which: a feature wave is a set of AI products released together, after a quick pass through Discover and Define; a user wave is the next group of users when a product scales (sub-step 5.7).
Quick passHow each new feature wave goes through Discover and Define after the first pass: four sub-steps always done (2.3, 2.4, 2.5, 2.7), the rest reused unless something changed, and gates 1 and 2 decided together in writing (Product Framework).
Material changeA change to a live product that goes back through step 4: new data, a new action, users outside the full user group step 4 approved, a new AI model we choose, or a vendor model change the vendor announces as major (smaller vendor updates are re-tested in 6.3). User waves inside the approved group aren't material. The product manager proposes and the risk partner confirms; the bank's model risk policy has the final word ⚠️ (Product Framework).
Higher-risk pathThe deeper step 4 review for a product whose output informs a credit decision, such as credit memo drafts: set at sub-step 2.4, under control K3.
PilotThe first live use, by a small pilot group, alongside a comparison group.
Comparison groupSimilar users who don't get the product yet, so its effect can be told apart from everything else that changed.
BaselineA measure's value before the product, recorded with its date, source and exact definition (sub-step 1.7).
Frozen versionThe exact version reviewers test and approve in step 4. Every fix creates a new frozen version, which is tested again.
Product machineThe whole system for running products: the six steps, the task tables (in product-machine.md, shown on the Product Machine page), the agents and the tracker.
PlaybookThe Product Playbook, the field manual: how to do each sub-step the first time, and what to log so it can be automated later.
First-time logThe notes kept while doing a sub-step for the first time: start and finish, time spent, what was done, inputs, waits, what repeated, the wait index, and the cut-first and then-automate answers (the fields are in the Product Playbook). Later projects keep a light version (start, finish, hours, waits) so the machine's measures can be counted every time.
Sub-step logThe log kept for every sub-step on every project: the full first-time log on the first project, then a light version (start, finish, hours, waits). The machine's measures and the wait index come from it.
Automation checkSeven questions asked of every Me and Draft task at each gate, in order: cut first (should it exist, can we delete it, simplify it, make it faster?), then automate what's left (did it repeat, is it stable, is it safe alone?). Three yeses to the last three move it one kind toward Auto. Law and bank policy are never cut.
Comes fromWhere a requirement (a control, sub-step or gate) comes from: law or regulator, bank policy or our choice. It says who to ask to change it: the partner who owns the law (compliance, privacy, model risk or vendor risk), the policy's owner or the product manager.
Limiting factorThe one thing holding a project back right now, with its owner (for example a data contract waiting for a signature). It opens the tracker and the weekly status note.
Wait indexFor a sub-step: calendar days from start to finish, divided by hours of real work, from the sub-step log. A high number means waiting, not working.
Machine measuresHow well the product machine itself works, per project: days from kickoff to pilot, days at each gate, releases per month in the pilot, and the wait index (Product Machine).
Gate in writingA gate decided by the decider approving the gate pack in writing, used when every criterion is met; a meeting is held only when a criterion is contested or someone who decides asks for one.
Use case kitThe standard set of pieces every use case should have, in two layers: the leader layer I communicate from (for bank leaders) and the working layer I and the team work from. Set by Prompt 1 in the Prompt Library, run first on RMs, and agreed 2026-10-17: 4 leader pieces (the case, the plan on a page, the value, the ask) and 10 working pieces. Cut on 2026-10-18: people get three short pages (an executive summary, the product and the plan), and every working piece goes into one detail document for AI to read (RM Detail for RMs).
Elon mode reviewA review of a plan or of the product machine through Elon Musk's lens (the Elon Mode page): the cut-first questions, the limiting factor and the machine's measures, plus the bank guardrails.

People, agents and safeguards

TermWhat it means
SponsorThe business leader who owns the outcome and the funding, and makes the call at each gate (together with risk at the step 4 gate).
Product managerThe person who owns scope, priorities and trade-offs, and owns the product until a long-term owner is named. Always spelled out: "PM" means portfolio manager.
RMs and PMsThe two user groups the work serves, always written this way: RM, RMs for relationship managers and PM, PMs for portfolio managers. Spell each out with its short form the first time on a page. An apostrophe shows ownership only ("the RM's own work"). (Decided 2026-10-14.)
Long-term ownerThe person who holds the product after launch, for example someone in a revenue line. Named in Define if known, confirmed before scaling (sub-steps 2.8 and 5.7).
Risk partnerThe model risk person who rates the product and works with the team from day one. Risk partners (plural) means everyone who signs off in step 4: model risk, compliance, privacy and security.
Data ownerThe person who decides what data may be used, and signs the data contract.
ChampionA respected user who shows peers how they use the product.
KindWho does a task: Auto (runs on its own), Draft (AI drafts, a named person checks), Me (only a person) or Partner (a decision only a named partner can make, such as a sign-off).
AgentAn AI helper with one job, a short list of allowed actions and a named person in charge.
Research helperAn agent that keeps my own reference pages current, such as H1, the bank news scout. Numbered H1 and on, listed in the Agent Library, and not part of the product machine.
General agentOne of the 33 agents (M1 to M33) that run the product machine on every project, specified in the agent library: M1 to M19 handle the paperwork and routing, M20 to M33 do the team's analysis, testing and monitoring.
Product agentAn agent inside the product that users see, numbered per project, each number used once across projects (A1, A2, A3 and A5 in the RM plan; A4 was merged into workflow B1 on 2026-10-18 and A6 removed on 2026-10-17; A7 to A11 in the PM plan, from 2026-10-19). Workflows are numbered the same way: B1 to B9 in the RM plan, B10 to B16 in the PM plan. Specified in that project's memo, Appendix A.
Agent specAn agent's written definition: its job, inputs, allowed and forbidden actions, outputs, when it stops, proof it worked and the person in charge.
Ongoing modeAn agent first used once in a sub-step that then keeps running until the product is retired (for example M24 on every data refresh). Agents built to run on a schedule from the start aren't counted as ongoing mode.
ControlA safeguard built into a product so the bank can trust it (K1 to K25 in the Controls Catalog). Each says where it is set, built, proved and watched, and where it comes from (law or regulator, bank policy or our choice).
Sign-offA named partner's formal approval in step 4. Never automated.
Quality barThe thresholds a product must meet: accuracy, sources shown, relevance, usefulness and permissions (zero tolerance). Set in Define.
Test setReal examples with known good answers, run against every version.
Red-teamingTrying on purpose to make the product fail: reveal other users' data, give a credit decision or go off-topic.
Made-up answerAn answer the AI states confidently that isn't supported by its sources ("hallucination").
DriftQuality slipping over time as data, models or instructions change.
BriefThe one-page product brief from Define: problem, outcome, users, platform, first-release scope, what's out of scope and the long-term owner.
BacklogThe ranked list of work still to build, with must-have controls at the top.
User storyA short "As a [user], I need [what], so that [why]" statement of something to build, with acceptance criteria that say how to tell it's done.
Release 1The smallest useful first version and the pilot group who get it (sub-step 2.8).
Assistive firstThe AI drafts or suggests and a named person decides. Autonomy grows only with evidence.
GuardrailA filter that blocks off-topic answers, harmful content or sensitive data.
OverrideA user changing or rejecting what the AI produced. Tracked as a risk and quality signal.
Monitoring planWhat is watched after launch, how often, the alert thresholds, who acts, and how to pause the product (sub-step 4.8).
Leading and lagging measuresLeading measures show up within weeks (use, insights acted on, pipeline created); lagging ones take months (closed revenue).
Data contractThe signed list of exact fields a product may use, how fresh they must be and the checks they must pass.
The five kinds of measuresUsage, quality, business impact, adoption, and risk and controls. Set in Define, tracked in Measure.
Value modelThe calculation of what a product earns against what it costs, with unknown numbers shown as [X]. The PM plan's model also counts costs saved and tracks loan losses avoided, not counted until Finance and credit risk agree a method (agreed 2026-10-19).

Pages and records

TermWhat it means
Designed pageA branded page a person reads. Everything a person reads is one.
Working copyThe Markdown file behind a designed page. people read the page.
Reference pageA page built to look things up: the Agent Library, Workflow Maps, Steps Catalog, Controls Catalog and this glossary (since 2026-10-18 they are the only pages in the Document Library's Reference section; the pages I read or act on sit elsewhere: the Job Description and Tech Stack Guide in "The bank and the role", the Use Case Library in Research, and Open Items, the Audit Log, the Prompting Guide and the Prompt Library in "My workspace"). RM Detail, in the RM section, and PM Detail, in the PM section, are built automatically for an AI to read, not for people. Claude keeps them aligned; they're never on the review list.
Gate packEverything the decider needs at a gate, put together in one document.
Evidence packEverything reviewers sign in step 4: the outputs of 4.1 to 4.6 (risk rating, data and privacy review, test and red-team results, fairness, controls and vendor reviews) and the draft monitoring plan (4.8).
Decision logThe record of each decision: what was decided, the options, who decided and why. For gates it also records the date the gate pack was ready and the decision date.
Risk and issue listThe one list of a project's dependencies, risks and issues, each with an owner and a date, reviewed weekly. Its top item is the limiting factor.
Project fileThe project's working document (memos/<project>.md), created at kickoff (1.0): the plan, the who-decides list and the approved AI tools. People read the short pages built from it; the full file goes into the project's detail document for AI.
Automation reviewThe table at the end of each playbook step that lists each sub-step's agent (or None) with its first-time kind and the kind to aim for; at the gate, the automation check runs through it.
TrackerThe one task list for a project (its section of the one shared tracker): each task with its owner, date and status.
Open itemAnything unfinished, kept in the Open Items log and numbered in priority order within its kind (W1 is the first piece of work). Numbers change when priorities change; closed items (C) keep theirs.

Bank terms to map

Our plans depend on these, but the definitions belong to the bank. Each stays ⚠️ until the bank's own definition is recorded here, with its source.

TermHow our plans use it todayBank definition
Relationship manager (RM)The banker who owns business client relationships and brings in new deals⚠️ to map
Portfolio manager (PM)The credit partner paired with an RM. Associated's own job postings use this title (AVP to SVP) for the role that underwrites, monitors credit and partners closely with RMs (seen on job boards, 2026-10-14)⚠️ to map
Credit officerThe person with credit authority who makes a credit decision that an AI draft or flag informs (control K3); it may be the portfolio manager or a credit approver⚠️ to map
Treasury management officerThe specialist an RM refers clients to for payments and cash management⚠️ to map
Client, relationshipA business client and everything the bank does with it⚠️ to map
Segment and marketHow clients and bankers are grouped (for example, by size or region)⚠️ to map
AI-sourced opportunityAn opportunity in the CRM that started from an AI insight, tagged so results can be counted⚠️ to map
Complete credit requestThe date a credit request has everything needed for a decision, the start of the PM plan's turnaround measure⚠️ to map
Win rateThe share of opportunities that close as won⚠️ to map
First-year revenue per winThe revenue a won deal brings in its first year⚠️ to map
Active user (weekly)A user who used the product in a given week⚠️ to map
