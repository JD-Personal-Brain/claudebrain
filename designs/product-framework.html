Decisions and trade-offs: record each one in the decision log, with the options, who decided and why, so partners stay aligned.

Gates in writing: when every criterion is met, the decider approves the gate pack in writing; a meeting happens only when a criterion is contested or a decider asks for one (Product Machine).

The risk and issue list: one list of dependencies, risks and issues, each item with an owner and a date, reviewed weekly. Its top item is the limiting factor: the one thing holding the project back right now, named with its owner. It opens the tracker and the weekly status note.

The quick pass for the next feature wave. After the first pass, each new feature wave (chosen in sub-step 6.5) goes through Discover and Define quickly, reusing what the project already has. A wave that serves a new user group starts a new project, with the full pass:

  • Always done: 2.3 update the data contract, and the data owner signs the new fields; 2.4 update the risk intake, and the risk partner confirms the rating; 2.5 user stories and acceptance criteria for the wave; 2.7 measures, quality bar and value model for the wave's AI products.
  • Reused unless something changed: 1.0 to 1.7 (record a baseline in 1.7 for any new measure, and show users a short 1.6 mock-up of any new AI product), 2.1, 2.2 (redone if the wave needs a different platform or delivery path), 2.6, and 2.8, where only the wave's scope and pilot group are updated. The product manager lists in the gate pack what was reused and what was redone.
  • Gates 1 and 2 together, in writing, from one short gate pack, with a meeting only if a criterion is contested.

The overlapping first pass (decided 2026-10-18, C55). On a project's first pass, gate 1 may be passed early in writing on a first sizing (1.2) and a provisional baseline, so building can start; the rest of Discover (interviews, the time map, the mock-up reactions, 1.3 to 1.6) keeps running, and the final baseline (1.7) is recorded before gate 2, which checks it. Comes from: our choice, so the pace is set by what really blocks us. The risk partner still agrees at gate 1, and no real client data is used before gate 2.

Change after launch. A material change goes back through step 4 before it reaches users: new data, a new action the product can take, users outside the group step 4 approved, a new AI model we choose, or a vendor model change the vendor announces as major. Smaller vendor updates trigger the 6.3 re-test (M32), and are judged as below. Step 4 approves the full planned user group from 2.8 (recorded in 4.7), so user waves inside it (5.7) are not a material change. The product manager proposes whether a change is material and the risk partner confirms. Any other change is re-tested (6.3), logged in the release notes and told to the risk partner. Comes from: our choice, resting on the model risk guidance's expectation that changes are reviewed. ⚠️ The bank's model risk policy has the final word on what counts as material.

The five kinds of measures

The main outcome measure is agreed in Discover (1.1) and its baseline recorded in 1.7; the full set is set in Define (2.7) and tracked in Measure and iterate. These are the five the job description names. Pick one or two of each per product, and define each one exactly (source, formula, owner) so the dashboard can be built from it.

KindWhat it tells usExample measures
UsageWhether people use what it gives themAnswers or insights opened and acted on; drafts and briefs used
QualityWhether its answers are right and usefulAccuracy on the test set, % of answers with sources, thumbs up or down
Business impactWhether it moves the outcome the sponsor cares aboutHours saved per user, cost or time per task, more client meetings, pipeline created, revenue won
AdoptionWhether it has become part of the routineWeekly active users, % of target users, repeat use week after week, use inside the routines managers run
Risk and controlsWhether it stays safeIncidents, human overrides, guardrail blocks, review findings

Leading measures show up within weeks (use, insights acted on, pipeline created); lagging ones take months (closed revenue). Report both, so a good start isn't mistaken for a result. The gate at the end of Release and adopt uses leading measures; lagging ones are judged in Measure and iterate.

The machine itself has its own measures (days from kickoff to pilot, days at each gate, releases per month, the wait index), on the Product Machine page.

The quality bar

How good is good enough. Thresholds are set in Define (2.7) as [X] until agreed. In Build (3.6 and 3.7) the test set checks accuracy, sources and permissions, and users grade a sample for relevance and usefulness. Step 4 confirms them on the frozen version.

TestWhat it meansThreshold
AccuracyNumbers and facts match the source[X]
Sources shownEvery answer links to where it came from[X]
RelevanceUsers rate it relevant to their work[X]
UsefulnessUsers would act on it[X]
PermissionsNo one sees data they shouldn'tZero tolerance

Gaps: only the sponsor (and, in step 4, the risk partner) can accept a documented gap on accuracy, sources shown, relevance or usefulness, recorded in the decision log with a date to close it. Permissions, and any threshold that comes from law or bank policy, can never be waived.

AI principles

These apply to every agent and every AI product, in every step.

  • Two kinds of agents at work on products. Some help the team do the work (the general agents in the Agent Library); others are the product users see (a project's own agents, in its memo's Appendix A). My research helpers (H1 and on) are a separate group: they also live in the Agent Library but aren't part of any product or the product machine.
  • Assistive first. AI drafts or suggests, and a named person decides. Autonomy grows only with evidence, through the automation check in the Product Machine (cut first, then automate).
  • Cut first, then automate. Every requirement says where it comes from: law or regulator, bank policy, or our choice. Law and bank policy are never cut; our choices are challenged at every gate before anything is automated.
  • One job each. Every agent has a clear job, a short list of allowed actions, read-only by default, and every action logged. Each one is written up as an agent spec before it's built.
  • Built where the work happens. Use the tools people already work in (Outlook, Teams, Salesforce), and add a new platform only when it's needed.

Starting a new project

Start every project with sub-step 1.0 in the Product Playbook (kickoff): name the outcome and the sponsor, fill in who's involved and who decides (above) with real names, copy the Product Machine's task tables into the shared tracker with an owner and a date for each task, brief the risk partner and create the project file, with this project's question, governance thread and gate for each of the six steps (from the Product Workflow). Then work through Discover with the playbook, writing the steps up in the table above. The plan's working copy is memos/<project>.md, as the RM memo does. People read three short pages (executive summary, product, plan); the full plan goes into the project's detail document for AI (RM Detail for RMs).
