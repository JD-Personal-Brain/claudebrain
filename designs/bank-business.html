1. Summary

  • The bank: $51.8 billion in assets at June 30, 2026 S1. It earns about 82% of its revenue from interest and 18% from fees (Q2 2026, my arithmetic from S2).
  • The corporate segment (the bank reports it as "Corporate and Commercial Specialty") made $177.3 million of revenue and $80.1 million of net income in Q2 2026, on $21.7 billion of loans, which is 60% of the bank's loans S2.
  • Its revenue lines: loan interest (general commercial lending, commercial real estate and specialty industries), deposits, treasury management fees, commercial cards, capital markets fees and loan fees. Fees were only $15.6 million, about 9% of segment revenue, in Q2 2026 S2.
  • ⚠️ Where AI work has the most room: selling treasury management and deposits to clients who only borrow, and giving a fast-growing team of relationship managers more selling time. Both fit the first wave of my RM analytics plan.
  • Watch: the bank's own announcement says its AI work in this business starts with portfolio management and treasury and payments S8. My value model has no line yet for costs saved or credit losses avoided.

3. How the bank earns money

A bank has two kinds of revenue:

  • Net interest income: the interest it earns on loans and investments, minus the interest it pays on deposits and borrowings. Q2 2026: $370.0 million S1.
  • Fee income (the bank calls it "noninterest income"): fees for services. Q2 2026: $80.4 million S1.

Four things stand out. All four are ⚠️ my reading of the numbers above:

  1. It is a lending business. Fees are 9% of the segment's revenue, against 20% in the consumer segment.
  2. It looks short of deposits. The bank charges each segment for the funds its loans use and pays it for the deposits it brings in. The segment paid a net $105.6 million for funding in Q2 2026, which suggests its loans are much larger than its deposits. Segment deposits are not public: [X]. If that is right, each new commercial deposit is worth more to this segment than the headline suggests.
  3. It is lean. Expenses were 33% of revenue. Of the $58.4 million, $24.6 million was the segment's own people and $29.5 million was its share of central costs (technology, risk, finance).
  4. Loan losses are a real cost. The $21.3 million provision is nearly as large as the segment's own payroll ($24.6 million).

7. Strategy and what's changing

  • The stated strategy has three parts: grow customer households, invest in talent to drive commercial growth, and speed up growth in major metro markets S13.
  • The C&I target was reached early. The bank reached its full-year target for organic C&I growth (9% to 10%) by June 30, 2026 S12. Management expects some C&I loans to be paid down in the second half.
  • New markets: a Kansas City commercial office opened in 2025 and doubled its team in March 2026 (from three bankers to six in its first year, per S9); a Dallas commercial office launched in May 2026; and Omaha is listed as a major growth market S3. ⚠️ I assume Omaha came with American National, which is based there. The bank named American National's former leader, Jason Hansen, as its Nebraska and Western Iowa market president on April 16, 2026, and he leads commercial banking there S16, which supports this.
  • American National: the deal closed on April 1, 2026. At March 31, 2026 American National had $5.2 billion of assets, $3.8 billion of loans and $4.5 billion of deposits, with 33 branches in Nebraska, Minnesota and Iowa S11. Systems, branches and accounts were due to convert in October 2026, and the cost-savings target is 30% of American National's expenses S3. Whether the conversion has happened: [X].

8. Risks and limits

Risks in the business:

  • It depends on interest income. 82% of bank revenue was net interest income in Q2 2026 (my arithmetic from S2), so earnings move with interest rates and loan growth.
  • Credit costs are rising from a low base. Loans written off were $23.2 million in Q2 2026 (0.26% of loans at a yearly rate), up from $5.3 million in Q1. Loans no longer earning interest were $150.0 million, or 0.41% of loans S1. Loans the bank is watching closely ("criticized loans") rose to $1.74 billion from $1.45 billion a quarter earlier S3. Management tied much of the increase to American National S12.
  • Concentration. Two-thirds of loans are commercial, and one industry (power and utilities) is 21% of commercial and business loans S3.
  • The integration competes for attention. ⚠️ A systems conversion this month means data, technology and risk teams are busy, and acquired clients' data may not sit in the same systems yet. That affects any product that needs clean client data.
  • Many new bankers. ⚠️ Fast hiring makes it harder to keep sales tracking and record-keeping in Salesforce consistent. It is also the reason capacity tools matter.
  • AI is a named risk. The FY2025 annual report includes a risk factor on the bank's use of AI and notes that states have started to regulate AI S4.

Limits of this briefing:

  • The split of fee lines between segments is public only once a year (FY2025, S5), and capital markets and loan fees are not split out at all.
  • The FY2025 annual report was too long to open in full. Its segment description and segment tables came through cut off, so I used the Q2 2026 quarterly report for segment figures and the bank's website for products.
  • Three sources are news summaries, not the bank's own documents: [S9], [S10] and [S12]. One news summary of the Q2 slides gave fee figures that disagreed with the earnings release, so I used the release and left that summary out.
  • The Q2 2026 earnings call transcript was not available to me, only a summary. As of October 14, 2026, third-party sites list a transcript, but the pages could not be opened from Claude's workspace.
