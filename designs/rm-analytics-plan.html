1 · THE OUTCOME

Two goals that work together

More revenue opportunitiesBetter insight into which clients to call, and why.
More time to act on themLess time on meeting prep, call notes and CRM updates. RMs at many commercial banks reportedly spend only about 25–30% of their time with clients.

New revenue can come from three levers. Finding out which is biggest is the first job.

01Win new clientsProspects that look like our best clients, and referrals from other parts of the bank.
02Grow existing clientsClients who need a product they don't have yet, such as treasury management, including referrals between teams (RM to treasury specialist).
03Keep clientsEarly signs a client may leave, such as falling balances or less activity.

CostsLicenses (for example, Copilot or Agentforce seats) + build effort + running costs (AI usage, data) + change management

How it's proven: pilot RMs are compared with similar RMs not yet using the tool, and every AI-sourced opportunity is tagged in Salesforce from day one. Finance agrees the method before the pilot starts.

5 · HOW WE DEPLOY AI

Small, single-purpose agents, with people in charge

Two kinds of agentsSome help the team do the work. Others are the product RMs use. Each is labeled.
Assistive firstAI drafts or suggests; a named person decides. Autonomy grows only with evidence.
One job eachA clear job, a short list of allowed actions, read-only by default, and a log of everything it does.
Built where the work happensSalesforce for client records, Teams and Outlook for daily work, AWS Bedrock and Snowflake for heavier AI and data.
Choosing the platform
  1. Where RMs spend most of their day
  2. What data the product needs, and where it lives
  3. Fit with the bank's existing approvals and controls
  4. Cost model (per user vs. per use)
  5. Speed to a working pilot
Starting point, to confirm in Discover: if RMs work mainly in Salesforce, show insights on the client record (Agentforce, Prompt Builder) with Snowflake as the data and signal engine, plus a weekly Teams or Outlook digest (Power Automate). Use one AI path; every extra platform adds build work and another review.

1Time-saving features draft call notes and CRM updates that RMs approve
2CRM data gets more complete
3Better data makes better insights
4Better insights earn more use, which produces more data
The loop compounds over time, and it's why both goals are built together.

APPENDIX B · WORKFLOWS

Each use case, step by step

Every flow has one decision point where the RM decides, and everything is logged.

B1Weekly opportunity listWave 1
Trigger: Every night (signals); every Monday morning (digest).
  1. 1A1 scans clients and writes signals.
  2. 2A2 writes an insight for each, shown on the Salesforce client record.
  3. 3A4 sends each RM their top [X].
  4. 4Decision point: the RM accepts, rejects (with a reason) or changes each insight.
  5. 5On accept, the RM creates a Salesforce opportunity tagged “AI-sourced.”
  6. 6A17 sorts the feedback; managers review the list in the weekly pipeline meeting.
Logged: Signals, insights, opens, decisions and reasons, opportunities created.
Measures: Share of insights acted on; pipeline from AI-sourced opportunities.
B2Pre-meeting client briefWave 1
Trigger: A client meeting on the RM's calendar, [X] hours before; or the RM asks.
  1. 1A3 pulls the client record, open insights and recent activity.
  2. 2It drafts a one-page brief with sources.
  3. 3The brief reaches the RM in Teams or Outlook.
  4. 4Decision point: the RM uses it and rates it with one click.
Logged: Briefs created, opened and rated.
Measures: Briefs used; prep time saved (RM survey); rating.
B3Meeting recap and follow-upWave 1 · first in the backup plan
Trigger: A client meeting ends.
  1. 1M365 Copilot produces the recap (from a recording only with client consent; otherwise from the RM's notes).
  2. 2A3 drafts the follow-up email and action items.
  3. 3Decision point: the RM edits and sends it themselves. Nothing is sent automatically.
Logged: Drafts created; drafts sent after RM edits.
Measures: Time to follow-up; drafts used; time saved.
B4Call notes into SalesforceWave 2
Trigger: The B3 recap is ready.
  1. 1A3 drafts the Salesforce activity note and next steps.
  2. 2Decision point: the RM approves or edits.
  3. 3On approval, the note is saved to Salesforce, tagged “AI-drafted.”
Logged: Drafts, approvals and edits.
Measures: Share of meetings logged in Salesforce (the data flywheel); admin time saved.
B5Early warning: client may leaveWave 2
Trigger: A1 finds a warning signal, such as falling balances or less activity.
  1. 1A2 explains the signal with the numbers.
  2. 2An alert appears on the client record and in the next digest.
  3. 3Decision point: the RM decides whether and how to reach out, and logs the action.
Logged: Alerts, RM actions, outcomes.
Measures: Clients kept against the comparison group.
B6Referral promptWave 2
Trigger: A1 spots a likely need another team serves, such as treasury management.
  1. 1A2 explains the need.
  2. 2Decision point: the RM clicks “refer” or dismisses it.
  3. 3On refer, Power Automate creates the referral in Salesforce and notifies the specialist.
  4. 4The specialist accepts it and the referral is tracked to close.
Logged: Prompts, referrals, acceptances, outcomes.
Measures: Referrals made and won.
B7RM assistant questionsWave 3
Trigger: The RM asks a question in Salesforce or Teams.
  1. 1A5 checks the RM's permissions.
  2. 2It answers from permitted data, with sources.
  3. 3Decision point: the RM acts; any record update needs their click.
Logged: Questions, answers, sources, updates.
Measures: Questions answered; answer ratings; zero permission breaches.
B8Prospect lookalikesWave 3
Trigger: Monthly. Compliance reviews the matching criteria before launch, because prospect selection can raise fair-lending questions.
  1. 1Snowflake finds prospects that look like the bank's best clients, using agreed and explainable criteria.
  2. 2A2 explains each match.
  3. 3Decision point: the RM decides whether to pursue each one.
Logged: Prospects shown, pursued and won.
Measures: New clients against the comparison group.
B9Every release (every week or two)All waves
Trigger: Any change to prompts, rules, data or models.
  1. 1A19 re-runs the test set.
  2. 2If the score is below the quality bar, the release stops.
  3. 3Decision point: if the change is material, the risk partner is told before release.
  4. 4Release; A19 watches live quality for [X] days.
Logged: Test scores, approvals, release notes.
Measures: Quality trend; releases rolled back.
