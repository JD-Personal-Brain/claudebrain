## Industry figures (directional only)
- **McKinsey (agentic AI, frontline):** banks that rewire one frontline area end to end report about 3–15% higher revenue per RM and 20–40% lower cost to serve. Based on a survey of about 400 US and Canadian bankers.
- **RM time:** RMs reportedly spend only about 25–30% of their time talking with clients (McKinsey, via Backbase).
- **Vendor claims:** 9% vs. 5% portfolio growth for RMs with AI workbenches, 10–12 hours a week saved, and 2× conversion on AI-generated lists. I couldn't trace these to a study.
- **Adoption:** McKinsey notes that training is infrequent, adoption of new tools fades, and traditional training rarely sticks.

## What it means for our framework
**The core holds.** Other banks follow the same pattern we planned: data-driven opportunity signals, delivered inside the CRM, with a reason shown and the RM in control.

**One meaningful change:**
1. **Pair insights with freed-up time.** The biggest reported gains come from rewiring the whole RM routine (prep, meetings, follow-up, CRM logging), not from insights alone. More opportunities don't help if RMs have no time to act on them. Add "banker capacity" (it's in the job description) as a partner to the revenue goal.

**Smaller tweaks:**
2. **Add "clients covered per RM"** to the measures (Bank of America's framing).
3. **Make adoption a management routine, not training.** Managers use the insights in pipeline meetings every week (already in Release; make it central).
4. **Capture the reason when an RM rejects an insight** (accept, reject or modify with one click).
5. **Consider internal referrals** (for example, RM → treasury specialist) under "win and grow."
6. **Our control-group measurement stands out.** Almost no published result uses one, so it's a strength to keep.
