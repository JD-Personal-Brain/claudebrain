            The five kinds of measures

            How we'd measure it

            How we'd know whether PMs use it, trust it and spend less time per review. The big numbers count what happened in this session, the tour included. Under each, a made-up sample pilot week (6 pilot PMs) shows what a real week might look like; in a pilot the numbers come from a live dashboard, compared with a group of PMs who don't have the assistant yet.

            How we'd get PMs to use it

            • Built into a routine managers already run: the annual review cycle and the portfolio review meeting (our assumption, to confirm with the bank).
            • Trained on their own work: each PM's first session uses a real review coming due in their own portfolio, not a demo.
            • Use, change or reject on every output: each click steers what gets better next.
            • Champions show peers how they use it, and wins (hours back, earlier warnings) get celebrated.
            • This tour is the first-time guide: it plays once, and anyone can replay it.

            The data

            • Sample borrowers: 12 made-up businesses for one PM, each with three years of statement figures, loans, covenants, deposits, payments, due dates, the last review and an RM note; plus 2 borrowers of another PM, used only to show the refusal. One borrower (Chequamegon Timber Works) is missing its FY2025 statements, to show the agents stopping rather than guessing.
            • Sample industry source pack: three industries (full-service restaurants, industrial distribution, metal fabrication), each with four made-up dated sources such as "Sample industry report, 2026". No real research firm, report or figure is used. It's the only thing A11 reads.
            • No outside data feed: everything the agents read is in the sample borrower file. An earlier version showed an outside interest-rate series as background, but it never entered a draft, so version 4 removed it. In production, our assumption (to confirm with the bank) is that the agents read certified data and the credit file from the bank's own systems.

            How it's tested

            Automated tests click through every promise on this page, with a stand-in for Claude: the tour, each agent, the industry section's sources and its refusal of borrower data and credit judgments, the guardrails (risky phrasings blocked and a PM's ordinary credit questions allowed), the judgment sections left blank, approval as final by the PM (the assistant never submits), the number check with planted made-up numbers, the missing-statement stop, laptop and phone layouts, and the recorded-run fallback. In a real build this grows into the test set and quality bar (K9), using real past reviews graded by experienced PMs.

            What a pilot would need (our assumption, to confirm)

            • Data: approval to use borrower financials and credit files for the pilot PMs' borrowers (the data contract). This usually sets the pace, not the build.
            • Review: drafts that inform a credit decision take the higher-risk path (K3): a fairness check, must-refuse tests and the credit policy owner's sign-off.
            • People: a sponsor, a risk partner from day one, the credit policy owner, a data owner, and a small pilot group of PMs with a comparison group.

            Where it sits in the plan

            This is the early clickable mock-up the product workflow calls for, built to test reactions. It follows the PM plan: The PM Assistant's wave 1 capabilities (the industry background draft, the borrower file and the review and memo drafts) and a wave 2 preview (early warning), agents A7 to A9 and A11, workflows B10 to B14 and B16, and controls K1, K2, K3, K4, K7, K8, K12, K22 and K25.

            Write the credit memo · workflow B16

            Industry background

            The Industry background drafter (A11) writes a first draft of a credit memo's industry section: the industry's size and trend, main drivers, main risks and outlook. Today an outside firm writes this section (details to confirm with the bank). Every sentence cites an approved source; you edit and approve it.

            Reads: approved industry sources only. No borrower data. It makes no judgment about any borrower: that analysis stays yours.

              In production (our assumption, to confirm): the source pack is the industry research the bank already licenses, approved by its content owner, and A11 runs on AWS Bedrock (Knowledge Bases), with no access to borrower data.
